Driving plan — Malaysia only

Three of you hold International Driving Permits, and Malaysia drives on the left like India. Self-drive covers Oct 19–22 (about 700 km in all); on Oct 23 a van with a driver handles Melaka, the border and the bags.

Vehicle

One 9-seat van

Hyundai Staria or Starex, booked in its 9-seat layout (driver + 8). Keeps everyone together with room for eight 30 kg bags, and stays within a normal car licence. About RM 200–270 a day.

Fallback: two 7-seat MPVs (Innova / Alza), four people and bags in each, two drivers

Drivers & papers

Three drivers

Each carries their Indian licence, IDP and passport. Register all three on the rental contract. The deposit goes on the main driver's credit card in the same name.

Mon: D1 port → Batu Caves, D2 → Cameron · Tue: D3 → Ipoh, D1 → KL · Thu: D2 → rafting → Genting, D3 night drive back

Road rules

Driving in Malaysia

Left-hand traffic. Seatbelts for every seat, rear included. 110 km/h on expressways, 60 or less in town. No handheld phones. Speed cameras are common.

Use Waze; tolls are cashless, so make sure the van has a loaded Touch 'n Go card

Parking & fuel

Before you drive off

Hill roads to Cameron and Genting are steep with fog after dark — use low gear downhill and swap drivers every 2 hours. Pick stays with parking that fits a ~2 m van. Malls charge RM 3–6 an hour. At petrol stations you pay at the counter first; foreigners pay the unsubsidised fuel price.

Option B

Drive all the way to Johor Bahru

Keep the van on Oct 23, stop at Putrajaya and Melaka, then return it in Johor Bahru (one-way fee ~RM 200). Rentals can't cross into Singapore, so the group then needs 2–3 cross-border taxis for 8 people and bags.

ComfortDelGro cross-border

Singapore

No rental car here

An MPV costs SGD 150–200 a day before road tolls and parking, and an 8-seater barely fits eight adults. The MRT plus a 13-seater maxi-cab for bag days is cheaper and easier.

Culture

Local etiquette

A few habits that keep things smooth for a group of eight.

Singapore

  • Fines are real: no eating or drinking on the MRT, no littering, no jaywalking, no vaping.
  • Hawker centres: a tissue packet on a seat means it's reserved ("chope"). Return trays and dishes to the racks.
  • Tipping: not expected; restaurants already add a 10% service charge.
  • Queues: people queue patiently for buses, taxis and food stalls — keep the group in single file.
  • Temples: remove shoes where signs say so at Sri Veeramakaliamman and Buddha Tooth Relic Temple; cover shoulders.

Malaysia

  • Batu Caves and mosques: cover knees and shoulders; sarongs can be rented at the entrance. Watch your bags around the monkeys.
  • Hands: give and receive money and food with your right hand; point with your thumb, not a finger.
  • Bargaining: fine at Central Market and street stalls, not in malls.
  • Tipping: not expected; rounding up for the van driver is appreciated.
  • Greetings: a smile and nod work best. During festival season, "Happy Deepavali" is welcome in both countries.

Staying safe

  • Singapore is very safe; the main risk is the heat — carry water and plan indoor afternoons.
  • KL: walk with bags on the side away from the road in Bukit Bintang; motorbike snatch theft happens.
  • Crowds: watch pockets at Jalan Alor, Petaling Street and the Batu Caves steps.
  • Taxis: use Grab or pre-booked vans; skip touts at the port and malls.

Emergency numbers

  • Singapore: 999 police · 995 ambulance and fire
  • Malaysia: 999 for all emergencies (112 from mobiles)
  • Meeting point: if anyone gets separated, regroup at the day's lunch spot and message the family group.
  • Keep handy: your aunt's address in Singapore and the KL Airbnb address, saved offline on every phone.
